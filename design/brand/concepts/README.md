# Logo concepts (Phase 5B, step 1)

Three original directions for the SMY-OS symbol, all paired with the same
drawn wordmark `SMY-OS`. Generated by `tools/design/brand_concepts.py` on a
100-unit grid; preview sheet: `make brand-preview` →
`build/brand/concepts-preview.png`.

**Status: decided — the owner chose A "Module" on 2026-10-21
([ADR 0011](../../../docs/decisions/0011-logo-concept-module.md)).** It becomes
the master symbol in step 2; B and C stay here as the record of the
exploration.

## Shared wordmark

Monoline geometric capitals, stroke 12 on a cap height of 80. `S` and `O`
share one corner radius family (the wordmark's signature: straight
horizontals ending in quarter turns, like the `S` signal path); `M` has its
middle vertex at 62 % of the cap height; `Y` joins at half height; the hyphen
is kept. Letters are paths, not a font — no font licence is involved.

## The concepts

| | A — Module | B — Signal | C — Prism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idea | A rounded-square frame (a window, an app, the system) whose top-right corner is completed by a separate accent square: the core module that the system is built around | One continuous orthogonal path tracing an S and flowing into a node: data moving to the core | A hexagonal frame with a Y of three arms meeting at a hexagonal core: modular structure, an isometric corner |
| Geometry | frame stroke 14, corner radius 20, core 23 × 23 with radius 5 aligned to the frame's outer edges | stroke 14, two radii equal to a quarter of the height, node Ø 22 on the path's end | frame and arms stroke 9, core radius 9 |

## Evaluation

Scores 1 (weak) – 5 (strong), judged on the rendered preview sheet
(dark, light, monochrome; 16, 32 and 64 px). Subjective design judgement,
written down so the decision is traceable.

| Criterion | A Module | B Signal | C Prism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recognition at 16 px | 5 — two clear masses (frame, core) | 4 — S readable, node merges with the stroke | 2 — thin strokes blur into a grey hexagon |
| Geometric simplicity | 5 | 4 | 3 |
| Memorability | 4 | 4 | 3 |
| Scalability (16 → 1024 px) | 5 | 5 | 3 |
| Monochrome | 5 — core stays separate by the gap | 5 | 4 |
| Fit for an operating system | 5 — reads as "window/app + core", works as app icon, boot mark, favicon | 4 | 4 |
| Distinctiveness from well-known marks | 3 — square with a corner "badge" resembles the notification-badge pattern of app icons; no specific mark found in a manual review | 3 — single-letter S marks are common | 2 — isometric cube marks are very common |
| Fit with the whole system (icons, UI) | 5 — rounded square + accent = the icon grid and the UI's accent rule | 4 | 3 |
| **Total (of 40)** | **37** | **33** | **24** |

Recommendation: **A — Module.** Strongest at small sizes and most natural
for an OS (app icon, boot screen, window corners), and it embodies the
"one accent colour" rule of the design quality bar. Weakness to address in
step 2: make the core's relation to the frame more specific (proportions,
gap) so it reads less like a generic notification badge.

The manual distinctiveness review compared the concepts against the logos of
Windows, macOS/Apple, Android, ChromeOS, Ubuntu, Fedora, Debian, Arch Linux,
elementary OS, Pop!_OS, Haiku, ReactOS and FreeBSD. It is not a trademark
search.
